The Chronicles

⚔ The World Above

Once there was a kingdom that built too deep. Its kings wanted more — more gold, more stone, more of the dark things that waited below the earth. They found what they were looking for. They found things that were not gold and not stone and did not want to be found. The kingdom is gone now. What it left behind are the three great descents — places where the boundary between the living world and what lies beneath it has grown thin enough to walk through. Brave people go down. Not all of them come back. The ones who do are changed.

💀 The Crypt of Malachar

Malachar was a warlord who believed that death was a problem to be solved. He spent fifty years searching for the answer. He found it in a burial vault beneath the eastern mountains — an ancient magic that could anchor a soul to the world of the living. He used it on himself. He did not read the fine print. The magic worked. Malachar did not die. He also did not live. He has been in the Crypt for a thousand years, perfectly preserved, perfectly aware, perfectly still, waiting for someone to arrive who is worth the effort of moving. His generals, his soldiers, and everything else that died in his wars is down there with him. They remember their oaths. They do not know why. They follow them anyway.

🔥 The Ashbound Wastes

The city of Vel Ashara was the greatest trading hub in the old world. It sat at the base of a dormant volcano that had not erupted in four hundred years. The city grew rich. Its people grew comfortable. The volcano was not dormant. It was thinking. On the night of the Burning, every fire in Vel Ashara went out at the same moment — and then everything caught fire at once. The city was swallowed in six hours. Something in the fire that consumed Vel Ashara was different from ordinary fire. It did not go out when there was nothing left to burn. It is still there now, three centuries later, burning the same ash it has been burning since the collapse. Explorers call the ruins the Ashbound Wastes. The locals, the few who remain in the hills above, call it by its older name: The Place The Fire Remembers.

❄️ The Frosthold Abyss

No one built the Frosthold Abyss. It was discovered by a mining expedition four hundred years ago when a shaft broke through the floor of the deepest tunnel and opened into a cavern so large that torchlight could not reach the far wall. The expedition descended. Their journals were returned to the surface three weeks later by unknown means. The expedition was not. The journals describe the cold — not as temperature, but as a presence. Something that waits at the bottom of the Abyss and is aware of the warmth of living things the way a drowning person is aware of air. Expeditions have been sent down every generation since. The journals always come back. The explorers never do. What is at the bottom of the Frosthold Abyss, no one who has seen it has survived to report. The journals stop before they get that far.

🟣 The Nightmare

There is a fourth place. It does not have a location. It is not beneath the earth or buried under ash or frozen in ice. It is the space between all three — the dark that exists between the dark things. Explorers who have descended far enough into any of the three dungeons sometimes report a moment where the dungeon changes. The walls breathe. The enemies are wrong. The silence sounds different. Most believe this is exhaustion or fear. A small number believe they have crossed into something the dungeons share — a deeper layer where the three places touch. If they are right, what lives there has never been named. The dungeon refuses to name it. So do the people who came back.

📚 The Sunken Archives

The Archives were not always underwater. There was a time — scholars argue about exactly when, because the Archives kept meticulous records of everything except the most important things — when the greatest library in the known world sat above ground, in a city whose name has been deliberately removed from every surviving document. The city was prosperous. The Archives were magnificent. Then the water came.

No one agrees on where the water came from. The leading theory involves the thing that was already living beneath the site when the foundations were laid. The architects knew it was there. They built around it. This was considered acceptable at the time. It was not acceptable for long. The thing below the Archives had been patient for a very long time. It simply had to wait until the library was complete. Until there was enough knowledge in one place to be worth consuming. Then it flooded the city from underneath and kept everything that drowned.

The books are perfectly preserved. The archivists who stayed are perfectly preserved. The thing that caused all of this is still down there, in the deepest chamber, surrounded by the collected knowledge of a civilization it ended. Explorers who descend report that the water is perfectly still. That the candles are still lit. That something has been marking certain pages. That the marks are in a language that did not exist before the flooding and has not been seen anywhere since. The Archives are still cataloguing. They have simply changed what they consider worth keeping.

🕯️ The Hollow

Nobody knows who built the house. This is unusual. Houses have builders. Houses have records. Deeds and blueprints and names on documents. The Hollow has none of these. The oldest maps of the region show the house already standing. The cartographers of the time noted this and moved on. Cartographers are practical people.

What is known is that families have lived there. Census records show occupants across several centuries, different names, different generations, all listing the same address. The records stop abruptly for each family. Not death records. Not moving records. Just a gap, and then new names, and then another gap. Nobody seems to have asked why.

The house is not haunted in the traditional sense. Haunted implies that something from outside came in and stayed. The Hollow is different. The Hollow is the kind of place where things that enter do not leave, but the house does not take them. They simply find, after a while, that they no longer want to go. The difference matters. The house has never forced anyone to stay. It has simply made staying feel more reasonable than leaving. One room at a time. One day at a time. Until leaving is a thing that happens to other people.

The Weeping Child has been there longest. Long enough that nobody remembers what she was called before she became what she is. The Weeping Mother arrived later, looking for something she lost. She has not found it. She has been there long enough now that she is not sure she would know what to do if she did. The house is patient. The house has always been patient. It has nowhere to be.

🌲 The Whispering Forest

The forest has always been there. This is the first thing the people who live near its edge will tell you, and they will say it with a particular tone — not pride, not fear, but the flat certainty of someone describing a fact they have long since stopped trying to explain. The forest was there before the villages. Before the roads. Before anyone thought to draw a map of this region. The trees are old in a way that old trees are not usually old. They do not look ancient. They look permanent.

The whispering started, as best anyone can document, about three hundred years ago. Or rather — that is when people began writing it down. The villagers suggest it has always been there too, that it is simply a thing you learn not to mention after a certain age, the way you learn not to mention other things. The whispers are not voices exactly. They are the shape of voices. The suggestion of words in a language that exists just outside the range of comprehension. Travelers who spend more than one night at the forest's edge report that the whispers begin to sound familiar. That they begin to sound like people they know. That they begin to sound like themselves.

The creatures inside the forest did not come from anywhere. They grew there. This distinction matters to the scholars who study the forest from a very safe distance. Things that come from somewhere can be driven back. Things that grew somewhere are a different problem. The forest is not a place where dangerous things live. The forest is a place that produces dangerous things the way other forests produce leaves, continuously and without particular intention. It is not malicious. It is simply what it is. It has been what it is for a very long time. It will continue to be what it is long after everyone who has ever entered it has been forgotten.

🔥 The Infernal Descent

There is a theology, practiced by a small number of scholars who do not advertise their beliefs, that holds that the underworld is not a place the dead go. It is a place that already exists, below the living world, and that the living world sits on top of it the way a house sits on a foundation. The foundation was there first. The house is temporary. The foundation is not.

The Infernal Descent is the place where this theory stops being theology and starts being geography. It is not a dungeon that was built or discovered or accidentally uncovered. It is a seam — a place where the surface of the living world has worn thin enough that what is underneath is visible. The heat that rises from the Descent is not heat from the earth. The earth does not produce heat like this. This heat has intent. It rises toward living things specifically. It has been rising for longer than the mountains above it have existed.

The Trial exists because the Descent must be earned. This is not a rule that anyone made. It is simply a property of the place. Explorers who have attempted to enter without preparation report that the entrance was not there. That they walked past it repeatedly and could not find it. That they only found it when they stopped looking and simply walked forward. The Descent chooses who descends. The criteria are not known. The results speak for themselves. One hundred trophies. One gate. What lies below the gate has no name that has survived the journey back to the surface. The explorers who returned said the words but the words were wrong by the time they arrived. The Descent keeps the real names for itself.

🌊 The Abyssal Deep

The ocean above the Abyssal Deep has no name on any map. Sailors who have crossed it do not speak of it afterward. Not from trauma — they simply find, on returning to port, that they have nothing to say. The crossing was uneventful. The water was dark. Something large moved beneath the surface at a distance that made estimation impossible. They arrived. This is the complete account of every sailor who has crossed those waters. The absence of detail is itself the detail.

The Deep is not underwater. This confuses people who have not been there. It is below the ocean floor — a space that should not exist, a cavern of impossible size beneath the weight of an ocean that has no awareness it is sitting on top of something alive. The water that fills the Deep is not ocean water. It is older than the ocean. It predates the concept of water having a surface. Things live in it that have never seen light and have developed, over an incomprehensible span of time, an extremely detailed understanding of the dark.

The Leviathan was not created. It was not born. It accumulated. Over millions of years, the Deep collected — pressure, cold, darkness, the memory of everything that sank and did not return — and at some point the collection became aware of itself. The Leviathan is not a creature that lives in the Deep. The Leviathan is what the Deep became when it had been alone long enough. Its four phases are not stages of a fight. They are layers of what it is — curiosity, recognition, fury, and finally the thing underneath all of those, which has no name, which the Leviathan saves for last, which it has shown to every explorer who made it to floor ten. None of them have found a word for it that works in any language. They all agree on what it felt like. It felt like being seen completely. For the first time. By something that had always been there.

❓ Help & Guide

Everything you need to know about Dungeon Descent. Use your screen reader heading navigation to jump between sections.

What is Dungeon Descent?
Dungeon Descent is a fully accessible turn-based dungeon crawler built specifically for screen reader users. You create a character, choose a dungeon, and descend through 10 floors fighting enemies, finding loot, and surviving long enough to defeat the final boss. Every element of the game is announced by your screen reader automatically.
How do I start?
From the main menu press Play. If you have no characters, press New Character. You will go through 12 creation steps — Class, Race, Profession, Name, Dungeon, Difficulty, Run Mode (Classic or Legacy), Spirit Companion, then four optional steps: Corruption Mode, Hunter Mode, Your Guide (name your companion), and Your Origin (backstory). Then you descend. The dungeon announces every room, every enemy, every event automatically.
How do I win?
Survive all 10 floors and defeat the boss on floor 10. The boss has 3 phases and gets stronger each phase. Defeat all 3 phases and the dungeon is complete. Completing a dungeon unlocks the next one.
What happens when I die?
A death cinematic plays — a unique line based on which dungeon you were in, which enemy killed you, and how deep you got. Then the run summary shows your stats. Your character stays on your roster unless you delete them. You can try again immediately.
What is a run?
A run is one complete attempt at a dungeon from floor 1 to floor 10. Each run your XP, gold, and skills reset. Your character persists between runs — their completion history, earned spirits, and journal entries carry over forever.
🎲 Run Modifiers
Every dungeon run has a chance to roll a random modifier before you descend — one of 8: Burning Dungeon (fire damage, +50% gold), Frozen Floors (enemies hit 20% softer, fewer rooms), The Watching (no event rooms, double XP), Death March (enemies 25% stronger, boss drops a legendary item), Gold Curse (triple gold, potions cost double), Phantom Run (invisible until you attack, guaranteed crit on your first strike), Berserk Mode (+40% damage, can't defend), and Blessed Run (start with a full inventory of potions and 100 bonus gold). Turn modifiers off entirely in Settings under Gameplay.
⏸ Pause Menu
Press Backspace on desktop or tap the ⏸ button in the dungeon top bar to pause. Shows your character name, current floor, dungeon, and HP, with options for Resume, Save, Settings, Quests, and Main Menu — all keyboard and screen reader accessible. You can't pause mid-combat; your screen reader announces why if you try.

Crypt of Malachar — Dungeon 1
The first dungeon. A thousand-year-old crypt ruled by the Undying King, Malachar. Undead enemies — skeletons, ghosts, wraiths. Standard dungeon mechanics. Recommended for all new players. The boss Malachar has three phases and summons adds in the final phase.
The Ashbound Wastes — Dungeon 2
A burning wasteland. Enemies deal fire damage. Some rooms have environmental fire hazards. The boss is The Eternal Pyre — an ancient fire entity. Unlocked after completing the Crypt. Harder than the Crypt, especially for classes with low defense.
Frosthold Abyss — Dungeon 3
A frozen abyss at the bottom of the world. Ice enemies that can freeze you in place. The boss is The Eternal Winter. Unlocked after completing the Wastes. Cold damage ignores some defenses — high HP classes recommended.
The Sunken Archives — Dungeon 4
An underwater library filled with drowned scholars. Unique lore fragment mechanic — find fragments scattered through rooms that build a complete story. The boss is The Head Archivist. Unlocked after completing the Abyss. Contains a unique secret room type.
The Hollow — Dungeon 5
An abandoned house. The strangest dungeon in the game. Has a weather system — every run rolls one of 5 weather conditions that change how the dungeon plays. Unique enemies include the Weeping Child (soothe it with the Music Box to unlock a later event), the Music Box itself, and the Uninvited Guest. The boss is The Last Resident.
The Whispering Forest — Dungeon 6
A village killed a witch named Seraphine three years ago. Her dying curse consumes everything inside the forest. Unlock by completing The Hollow.

Corruption Bar — a green bar fills as you descend. It increases passively over time AND when enemies hit you. Reach 100% corruption and you die instantly regardless of HP or difficulty.

Music Box Item — drops every 3 floors. Go to your inventory and activate it manually to clear all corruption and play the melody. Plan ahead — if you miss a Music Box you may not survive.

Potions — in the Whispering Forest potions restore HP AND reduce corruption by 30%. Every potion decision matters more here.

Final Boss — Seraphine the Undying or The Corrupter. The game picks randomly each run. You never know which one is waiting on floor 10.

The Corrupted Chimera — guaranteed on floor 9 your first visit. Three corrupted parts each with unique mechanics.
The Infernal Descent — Dungeon 7
A dungeon of pure void and flame. Requires 100 trophies to unlock — the game checks automatically and unlocks it when you reach that threshold. Features void entities, infernal creatures, and devastating bosses across 3 tiers. Has its own music, ambient track, and weather system.
The Abyssal Deep — Dungeon 8
The bottom of the ocean where no light reaches. Unlocks automatically after completing The Infernal Descent. Features 9 unique ocean enemies — Tide Wraith, Drowned Sailor, Ink Fiend, Deep Stalker, Siren, Abyssal Eye and more, each with unique combat mechanics. The Leviathan is the final boss with 4 phases and 800 HP — the hardest boss in the game. Each phase transition plays a different music track. Has 5 unique dungeon events including The Witness — a shark that has been watching you the entire run and becomes your permanent minion if you earn its trust. Has its own weather system, music, and ambient. Bring everything you have.
The Shadow World — Dungeon 9
A secret dungeon that has existed for 3000 years beneath a cursed forest. 14 enemies with unique mechanics, including The Mimic, The Lurker, The Reflection, and Shadow Archon. Shadow Corruption builds per hit you take — at 3 stacks your damage is halved, so spend the 4 SP on Purge before it gets there. Shadow Ambush encounters bring back echoes of enemies you already killed this run, each adding more corruption on hit. The Echo always spawns first as the floor 10 boss; defeating it auto-triggers a dramatic fight against The Shapeshifter, a 4-phase boss that copies your weapon damage, matches your exact HP, and corrupts you on every hit — widely considered the hardest boss in the game. A Security Room event can also appear: watch 4 camera feeds for the Darkness Swallower and escape the opposite direction, by sight or by its growl, before a 15-second timer runs out.
Dungeon Weather System
Every dungeon run rolls a random weather condition at the start. Each dungeon has its own weather pool. Effects include enemy ATK multipliers, gold boosts, HP loss per room, SP discounts, and healing per kill. The condition is announced by the screen reader and shown in the weather banner at the top of the dungeon screen.
Hollow Weather System
Every time you enter The Hollow the game rolls one of 5 weather conditions. Foggy Night adds 15 percent combat chance. Full Moon makes enemies 20 percent stronger but gold drops 50 percent more. Silent Night doubles Weeping Child spawn rate. The Witching Hour gives you 25 percent more damage but enemies crit more. Still and Cold has no effect — the house is just watching.

New Game Plus
Clear any dungeon and a choice screen appears: Continue Journey (move on to the next dungeon with your current character) or New Game Plus (replay that same dungeon, harder, keeping your character and spirit). Each dungeon tracks its own NG+ count — enemies get roughly 20 percent stronger on your first NG+ run through a dungeon, with each subsequent NG+ adding about 5 percent more on top. There is no cap.
Hunter Mode
Enable Hunter Mode at character creation Step 9 or toggle it from the pause menu. Once active, a relentless hunter enters the dungeon behind you. It moves every time you move or kill an enemy. If you idle for 3 or more turns it rushes — moving 2 rooms per turn. The distance is announced every room change via your screen reader and shown on the hunter tracker bar at the top of the screen. At 5 rooms away the heartbeat sound begins and speeds up as it closes in. At 3 rooms away the music shifts to a dark tension track. At 2 rooms away a Fight or Run choice appears — you can face the hunter in a scaled boss fight or sprint away and buy one more turn. If you kill the hunter you earn the Hunter's Trophy item and Hunter Mode deactivates for the rest of the run. If it catches you — jump scare. A 💀 HC badge shows in the top bar when Hunter Mode is active.
Hunter Mode — Survival Tips
Keep moving — the hunter only catches you if you idle. Clear rooms quickly and always move after winning a fight. The mercy distance resets slightly when you advance a floor so floor transitions buy time. Fight the hunter at level 10 or above for the best chance — it scales with your level but so do your stats and skills. If you choose to run at 2 rooms it resets to 1 room — you only bought one turn so keep sprinting. The heartbeat speed tells you how close it is — when it hits maximum speed at 400ms intervals you are 1 room away.
Shadow Realm
The Shadow Realm is a hidden secret mode. To access it go to Settings, tap Redeem Code, and type SHADOW. A dramatic intro screen will appear and then character selection. The Shadow Realm is an endless wave survival mode — each wave spawns shadow entities that grow stronger over time. Every 10 waves a boss wave appears. Your score is tracked on a local leaderboard so you can compete against your own best runs. Void Reaper music plays throughout. The Shadow Realm does not save progress — each run starts fresh.
Boss Fights — Strategy Guide
Every dungeon boss has 3 phases. Each phase transition makes the boss stronger and changes its attack pattern. A boss preview screen shows the boss name and HP before combat begins — read it carefully. General tips: save at least 2 potions before floor 10. Use your strongest skill in phase 1 while the boss is weakest. Defend during phase transitions to reduce the burst damage. If you have a spirit active use its combat skill every cooldown. The boss scales with your player level so higher level characters face stronger bosses — there is no free ride.
Basic Combat Actions
Attack — always hits, deals standard damage. 10 percent chance to crit for 1.8x damage.
Heavy Strike — deals 1.7x damage but 30 percent chance to miss completely.
Defend — reduces damage this turn and deals a small counter hit.
Use Skill — activates one of your unlocked class skills. Costs SP.
Soul Trap — appears in combat when you have a Soul Trap in your inventory. Captures the current enemy as a permanent minion. Cannot be used on bosses.
Flee — attempt to escape combat. Success chance depends on your race and profession.
Elites and Bosses
Elite enemies appear on floors 4 and above. They have much higher HP and special abilities. Each dungeon has its own elite enemies. The boss appears on floor 10 and has 3 phases — each phase changes its attack pattern and gets stronger. The Secret Boss (DDDI) has a 2 percent spawn chance on floor 10 of any dungeon.

Spell System
Spells are a separate combat system from skills. To use spells you need two things — a Spell Book and spell scrolls. The Spell Book is purchased from the Arcane Shop for 120 gold and unlocks your ability to learn spells. Spell scrolls are also bought from the Arcane Shop and cost between 40 and 150 gold each depending on rarity.

Each class has a spell learn chance — the probability that reading a scroll teaches you the spell. Some classes like Wizard and Elementalist have higher learn chances. If a scroll fails to teach you the spell it is still consumed.

Spells have a 3-turn cooldown after use. Powerful spells — marked with a special tag — have a 6-turn cooldown and are significantly stronger or more dangerous.

To cast a spell in combat press the Spells button or use Alt+S. A list of your learned spells appears. Select one to cast it. Spells work in normal dungeon combat, The Spire, Raids, and ambush multi-enemy battles.
Spell Book Screen
The Spell Book screen shows every spell you have learned on your current character. Access it from the main menu, the Arcane Shop, or the dungeon action bar using the Spell Book button. In the dungeon you can also open it with Ctrl+Shift+K.

Each spell entry shows its icon, name, rarity, full description, and cooldown type. Common and rare spells show a 3-turn cooldown. Powerful legendary spells show a 6-turn cooldown. The screen also shows your class name and current spell learn chance at the top.
Spell Rarities and Risk
Spells come in three rarities — common, rare, and legendary. Common spells are reliable but modest. Rare spells deal higher damage or apply status effects. Legendary spells are the most powerful in the game but many are double-edged — they can backfire catastrophically if you are not careful.

Examples of high-risk legendary spells: Shadow Blast summons a void spirit that can instantly kill you if it rejects you. Time Stop freezes time for 3 free attacks but has a 50% chance of defeating you instantly if it fails. Lose Reality gives you 5 minutes to kill the enemy or you cease to exist — a real countdown timer begins. The Abyss Stares Back has a 40% chance to halve the enemy attack permanently and a 60% chance to halve yours permanently with no way to undo it.

Powerful spells are excluded from ambush multi-enemy target selection and apply to the first living enemy automatically.
Ambush and Multi-Enemy Combat
On floor 3 and above there is a chance of an ambush — 2 or 3 enemies attack at once. During an ambush the normal single enemy display is replaced by a multi-enemy tracker showing every enemy's name, HP bar, and current HP.

When you choose Attack, Heavy, or a skill the game asks you to choose your target first. A list of living enemies appears — select one to strike it. Defend and Potion do not require a target and apply immediately. Flee has a 40% success chance in ambushes.

After your action every living enemy counter-attacks in sequence. The Goat Body part of the Chimera regenerates HP each turn. The Serpent Tail poisons you each turn. Plan your target priority carefully.

Skills and spells both work in multi-enemy combat. Buff skills like Divine Shield, Marked Kill, and Inner Focus apply to you immediately and then enemy turns proceed. Offensive skills go through the target selector. Spells target the first living enemy automatically.

During Ambush Day — which occurs four times a year on the 15th of January, April, July, and October — every single battle in the game becomes a multi-enemy ambush for 24 hours.

🏹 General Survival Tips
Never skip the first shop. A potion on floor 1 can save your run on floor 7. Always keep at least 1 potion in reserve for the boss fight. Search every room before moving — searched rooms can reveal hidden loot. Flee is not failure — sometimes running is the smartest move. Defend when your HP drops below 30 percent to cut incoming damage in half.
⚔ Class Strategy Guide
Warrior and Paladin — attack every turn, use Heavy Strike when HP is high. Tank hits, never flee. Mage and Sorcerer — save SP for elites and bosses. Use basic attacks on weak enemies to conserve. Rogue and Assassin — open every fight with a skill for the first-hit bonus, then spam crits. Cleric — heal between floors using skills, save potions for emergencies. Necromancer — capture a minion early and keep it alive all run. Ranger — always open with your strongest skill for the double damage first hit bonus. BloodKnight — stay at medium HP to maximize lifesteal, never fully top off.
🐉 Boss Fight Strategies
Enter floor 10 with at least 2 potions and full HP. Use your strongest skill immediately in phase 1 — the boss is weakest here. When phase 2 triggers defend on that turn to absorb the spike damage. In phase 3 go all out — use every skill you have. If your spirit has a combat ability use it every single cooldown. The boss preview screen tells you the boss HP — plan your skill rotation before you press Face It.
🏹 Hunter Mode Survival
The key is momentum — never idle. Kill enemies fast and keep moving. Use floor transitions strategically — advancing to the next floor resets the hunter distance slightly. Fight the hunter only at level 8 or above. Before the fight make sure you have full HP and at least 1 potion. When choosing between Fight and Run — if you have a strong skill ready, fight. If your HP is below 50 percent, run.
💰 Gold and Economy
Prioritize potions over weapons early game — surviving matters more than damage output on floors 1 through 5. On floors 6 through 9 invest in weapon upgrades at the Blacksmith to spike your damage for the floor 10 boss. Soul Traps are worth buying on any run — a permanent minion companion adds free damage every fight for the rest of the run. Never spend your last gold on something optional before the boss floor.
⚗ Building a Strong Character
Pick a class that matches your playstyle first. Then choose a race that amplifies it — Human for balanced stats, Elf for SP and skills, Dwarf for HP and defense. Choose a spirit that covers your class weakness — a healing spirit on an aggressive class, a damage spirit on a defensive class. At level 5 choose your subclass carefully — check both options and pick the one whose passive works with how you already play. Upgrade your weapon at least twice before floor 8.
🌑 Shadow Realm Strategies
The Shadow Realm gets harder fast — focus on survival not speed. Defend every 3 turns to recover HP since there are no potions. Prioritize killing the weakest enemies first to build your score safely. On boss waves switch to full offense — they have high HP but the wave ends when you kill the boss. The longer you survive the higher your leaderboard score — every wave counts.
💀 Hardcore Mode Tips
In Hardcore Mode every decision is permanent. Start on Wanderer difficulty your first hardcore run — do not combine Hardcore with Nightmare until you know the game deeply. Never fight elites without full HP. Always have a potion before floor 7. Flee aggressively — your character's life is worth more than the gold from one fight. The hunter is a death sentence in Hardcore — either ignore Hunter Mode entirely or fight the hunter the moment the choice appears before it can catch you off guard.
🏆 Road to Dungeon Master
To unlock The True Ending you need to clear all 8 dungeons — progress is saved across all characters. A smart route: start with Crypt of Malachar on Wanderer to learn the basics. Then Ashbound Wastes, Frosthold Abyss, Sunken Archives in any order. The Hollow is the hardest mid-game dungeon — do it last in the first half. Save The Infernal Descent and The Abyssal Deep for when your characters are experienced. The True Ending rewards patience, not speed.

On first launch the game asks if you are using a screen reader. Pressing Yes enables all accessibility features at once — Verbose Mode, Screen Reader Headings, Combat Announcements, Reduced Motion, and Screen Reader Mode. You can also enable or disable each feature individually in Settings under Accessibility.
Screen Reader Headings
When enabled in Settings, the game injects heading roles throughout every screen. H1 for screen titles and dungeon floor names. H2 for section titles, menu buttons, tabs, character names, and enemy names. H3 for individual bestiary entries, achievements, library stories, and skill names. With headings on you can press H in JAWS to jump instantly between sections instead of tabbing through everything.
Verbose Mode
When Verbose Mode is on your screen reader announces extra detail about every action — full damage numbers, miss explanations, skill descriptions when used, item descriptions when found. When off announcements are shorter and faster. Recommended on for new players, can be turned off once you know the game well.
Combat Announcements
When on your screen reader announces every combat event in detail — your damage, enemy damage, crits, misses, status effects, skill activations. When off only the most important events are announced. Combat Announcements is separate from Verbose Mode — you can have one on and the other off.
Custom Track and My Tracks
Go to Settings → Audio → scroll down to find the Custom Track section.

Load from File — tap Choose Audio File and pick any audio file from your device. Works with MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG and more. iOS users can pick from the Files app. The track plays immediately and you are asked if you want to save it.

Load from URL — paste a direct link to an audio file hosted online. The URL is saved permanently so it loads automatically next time.

Save to My Tracks — when a custom track starts playing the game asks what you want to call it. Give it a name like Leo's Battle Theme or Self Destruct Sequence 123 and it saves to My Tracks. Files are stored on your device using IndexedDB — they survive app restarts and do not count against the game's storage.

My Tracks — found at the bottom of the Music Player. All your saved tracks appear here. Tap one to play it. Tap the X button to delete it. Custom tracks loop continuously just like the game soundtrack.
Music Player
Found in The Chronicles under the Music tab. Browse all 29 tracks in the game grouped by dungeon. Select any track and press Play — the music player uses its own audio system separate from the game so it works from the menu without needing to enter a dungeon first. Press Stop to stop playback. The player also tells you which audio files are missing if you have not added them yet.
Reduced Motion
When on disables ember particle effects, grain overlay animation, vignette animation, and torch flicker. Reduces visual noise for players who prefer a cleaner interface or have motion sensitivity. Has no effect on gameplay.
Endless Descent — AFK Mode
A separate game mode found in the main menu. Auto-combat that runs on its own — your character fights while you watch or step away. Infinite floors with scaling enemies. Find loot, level up, earn Descent Points to spend in the AFK shop on permanent upgrades. Boss floors every 25 floors. Daily AFK Challenge for bonus points. Exclusive enemies appear on floors 20 and beyond that only exist in Endless Descent.
Tournament
A seasonal competition that runs every 3 months for 7 days. A unique boss appears and you fight it for score. Damage dealt plus speed bonus plus survival bonus equals your tournament score. Damage counts even if you die — your best score this season is always saved. Win the boss for maximum points and earn Mystery Coins. Global leaderboard shows top 10 scores. Requires an account to appear on the leaderboard.
The Void Reaper
A world event that builds over 7 days. A progress bar appears on the main menu as the Void Reaper awakens. When fully awake a 300 HP boss fight becomes available. Players get a strength bonus. Damage carries over between attempts — every hit counts even if you die. 3 phases, each stronger. Defeat him in 16 hours or all dungeon enemies are 25% stronger for 24 hours as punishment. Win to earn Mystery Coins.

Welcome — Step 1 of 8

Welcome to Dungeon Descent

You are about to descend into one of the darkest games ever made for screen reader users.

Dungeon Descent is fully accessible. Every button, every enemy, every room description — all announced by your screen reader automatically.

This quick guide will show you how everything works. You can skip it anytime.

💡 Built for JAWS and VoiceOver
Every action in the game is announced. Combat damage, loot found, floor descriptions — your screen reader reads it all.

Character Creation — Step 2 of 8

Building Your Character

Before you descend, you build your character across 10 steps.

Class — 18 classes available. Warrior, Monk, Assassin, Paladin, Naturalist, Sorcerer, Necromancer, Berserker, Bard, Alchemist, BloodKnight, Druid, Warlock, DeathKnight, Ranger, Sentinel, Cleric, Wizard. Each plays completely differently.

Race — 11 races. Human is balanced. Orc hits hardest. Vampire heals on every kill. Angel gains a second life. Goblin finds 25% more gold. Merfolk get bonus HP in the Sunken Archives.

Profession — 11 options. Herbalist doubles potion healing. Pilgrim gets 15% bonus XP. Blacksmith gets 10% off upgrades.

Starting Skill — every class now begins with their signature skill already unlocked on floor 1.

💡 Tip for new players
Start with Warrior class, Dwarf race, Herbalist profession on Wanderer difficulty. This gives you the most survivability for your first run.

Navigation — Step 3 of 8

Moving Through the Dungeon

The dungeon has 10 floors. Each floor has 5 rooms. After room 5 you reach a shop, then descend to the next floor.

Use the North, South, East, West buttons to move between rooms. Direction does not matter — each move generates a new room.

Use the Search button to search the current room for hidden loot. You can only search each room once.

Every room can contain combat, loot, a special event, or nothing. You never know what waits ahead.

💡 Special Events
About 1 in 10 rooms triggers a special event — an ancient shrine, a mysterious chest, a wounded adventurer, or an inscription. These give rewards if you interact with them.

Accessibility — Step 4 of 8

Features Worth Turning On

Your screen reader announcements work automatically — nothing to enable there. But a few opt-in features are easy to miss.

Voice Commands — a microphone button appears during exploration and combat. Say things like "attack," "north," or "defend" to play hands-free. Enable it in Settings.

Haptic Feedback — distinct vibration patterns for hits, crits, level-ups, and danger. On by default — adjust intensity in Settings under Accessibility.

3D Mode & Echo-Location — an optional spatial-audio layer for exploring rooms. Turn it on in Settings under Beta Features, then use the Echo-Location button (or Alt+E) to sonar-ping a room for walls and doors.

💡 Command Center
Prefer typing? The Command Center lets you play the entire game — including character creation — with text commands instead of buttons.

Combat — Step 5 of 8

Fighting in the Dark

Combat is turn-based. You act, then the enemy acts.

Attack — reliable, always hits. 10% chance to critically hit for 1.8x damage.

Heavy Strike — deals 1.7x damage but has a 30% chance to miss completely.

Defend — reduces incoming damage this turn and deals a small counter hit.

Potion — drinks a health potion from your inventory. Herbalists get double healing.

Skills — unlock as you level up. Each class has unique powerful abilities that cost SP.

Soul Trap — if you have one in your inventory, a Soul Trap button appears in combat. Use it to capture the enemy as a permanent minion instead of killing it.

🗡 Try it yourself
A harmless Training Shade will appear. Practice Attack, Heavy Strike, and Defend against it — it can barely scratch you, and you cannot actually die to it.

Progression — Step 6 of 8

Growing Stronger

You earn XP from every kill. Level up to gain HP, attack power, and skill unlocks. Every level gives 3 points to spend on stats.

SP (Skill Points) power your class skills. You start with 20 SP and regenerate 2 per room entered.

The shop appears between floors. Buy potions, weapons, armor, runes, scrolls, and Soul Traps with gold from kills.

Complete dungeons to unlock the next. 8 dungeons — Crypt, Ashbound Wastes, Frosthold, Sunken Archives, The Hollow, Whispering Forest, The Infernal, and The Abyssal Deep.

Trophies — earned from achievements. Reaching 100 trophies unlocks The Infernal Descent.

💡 Minions and Forge
Use a Soul Trap in combat to capture enemies as permanent minions. Collect materials from kills and craft custom weapons at the Forge inside your Home. Both are accessed from the Home screen between fights.

Blacksmith — Step 7 of 8

The Blacksmith

The Blacksmith appears inside dungeons and lets you do two things.

Upgrade — spend gold to upgrade your currently equipped weapon or armor. Higher level gear deals more damage and has better defense.

Buy Weapons — browse a selection of weapons available for purchase with gold. Your first weapon is always free — a starter blade to get you going.

Weapons you buy go straight into your inventory. Open your inventory to equip them.

💡 Blacksmith Profession
If you pick Blacksmith as your profession during character creation, you get a 10% discount on all upgrades. Great for long runs.

Ready — Step 8 of 8

You Are Ready to Descend

That is everything you need to know. The rest you will learn in the dark.

Check the Bestiary to track every enemy you encounter. Visit The Chronicles for lore and stories. Use Quests for daily challenges and achievements.

The Blacksmith is on the main menu — upgrade weapons between runs using your global gold. The Daily Raid gives gold and Mystery Coins. The Spire lets you climb 1800 floors endlessly.

Every achievement earns you gold and trophies. 100 trophies unlocks The Infernal Descent. Unlock The Abyssal Deep after that.

Good luck. The dungeon has been waiting.

🧊 3D Settings

Graphics and performance options for 3D Mode. Lower settings run smoother on older phones; higher settings look better on newer ones.

Shadows
Real-time torch shadows. Looks better, costs more performance — off by default since it's the single heaviest setting here.
Torch Flicker
Animated flickering torchlight. A small, mostly-free effect — worth leaving on unless you're really scraping for performance.
FPS Counter
Shows a live frames-per-second counter, useful for judging whether other settings here are actually helping.
Minimap
A small top-down map of rooms you've explored. Only ever shown for sighted play — screen reader sessions never display it.
